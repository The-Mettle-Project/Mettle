processes, and what the kernel keeps for each one:

  a pid that is never reused, the pid of its parent, a state, a priority, the
  stack it owns, the pages it claimed, how many ticks it has burned, how many
  times it has been switched to, when it started, and the code it exited with

states: ready, running, sleeping until a deadline, blocked on a channel,
zombie until its parent collects it, free

the scheduler runs on the timer, a thousand times a second. it charges the
running process a tick, wakes any sleeper whose deadline has passed, and when
the running process has spent its quantum it picks again. it takes the highest
priority process that is ready, round robin inside that level, except that
anything ready and passed over for a quarter of a second is treated as top
priority so nothing starves. when nothing is ready the idle process halts.

sleeping is real sleeping. a process that sleeps or blocks is skipped by the
scheduler until its deadline passes or someone wakes its channel, so it costs
nothing while it waits. the shell blocks on the terminal key channel, and the
keystroke that arrives wakes it.

exiting is real too. a process that exits becomes a zombie holding its exit
code, its stack is handed back on the next switch, and the pages it claimed are
released. a parent reads the code with wait, and the idle process collects the
zombies whose parent is already gone.

processes talk to each other by mail. every process has a small mailbox, and a
send wakes it if it was blocked waiting on one. a process that exits hands its
children to pid one first, so nobody is left pointing at a parent that is gone.

each stack carries a guard word at its lowest address. the scheduler checks it
every time it switches away from a process, and a process that has run off the
bottom of its own stack is stopped and reported rather than left to corrupt
whatever it landed on.

try it from the shell:

  ps            every process, with its state, priority and processor share
  top           the same, ordered by processor share
  work 3000     a process that burns the processor for three seconds
  wait 7        block until that pid exits, then read its code
  spawn 3       three counters that wake ten times a second
  nice 8 low    move one down a priority
  kill 8        ask one to stop
  mailbox       a process that blocks until mail arrives
  send 9 5      post it a number, and zero to make it exit
